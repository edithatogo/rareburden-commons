# Vision, mission and purpose

**Status:** Founding draft v0.1  
**Date:** 18 July 2026

## Vision

> **A world in which no person living with a rare disease is invisible in the evidence that shapes health policy, investment and care.**

The vision is not merely better datasets. It is an international decision environment in which the collective consequences of rare diseases can be compared with other health priorities without erasing diagnostic complexity, uncertainty or lived experience.

## Mission

> **To make the collective health, social and economic burden of rare diseases measurable, comparable and actionable by connecting existing evidence, developing transparent methods, building distributed analytic capacity and translating results into decisions.**

The mission has four verbs:

1. **Connect** fragmented public, controlled and partner-held evidence.
2. **Measure** burden using explicit estimands, assumptions and uncertainty.
3. **Distribute** analytic capability so sensitive data can remain with custodians.
4. **Translate** evidence into policy, service design, research priorities and investment.

## Purpose

RareBurden Commons exists to correct a structural measurement problem: rare diseases are commonly studied one condition at a time, while global burden systems, administrative classifications and funding decisions operate through broad cause groupings. The result is collective invisibility, overlap, miscoding and an inability to identify rare aetiologies embedded within common clinical categories.

The programme's purpose is to provide a trusted measurement layer that:

- describes what is already known and where evidence is missing;
- estimates the collective burden of rare diseases without simple, invalid summation;
- quantifies rare causes within common disease envelopes;
- exposes diagnostic, geographic and socioeconomic inequities;
- measures consequences for people, families, health systems and economies;
- supplies reproducible evidence for policy and funding decisions;
- allows existing data custodians to contribute without surrendering control of sensitive records.

## Founding proposition

> Rare diseases are individually uncommon, collectively consequential and systematically under-measured. A public-data-first, federated and patient-governed measurement system can make that burden visible without constructing another international patient database.

## Public value

The programme is intended to create public infrastructure rather than a proprietary analytic service. Its core definitions, metadata, protocols, code and aggregate outputs should be openly reviewable wherever licences and disclosure rules allow.

## Values

### People before datasets

People living with rare diseases and their families are decision-makers, not merely data sources. Their priorities, acceptable-use boundaries and interpretations must shape the programme.

### Global fairness

The absence of data is not evidence of the absence of burden. Methods must avoid treating high-income, well-coded populations as the universal reference and must fund locally led participation.

### Scientific humility

Uncertainty, heterogeneity and missingness will be made visible. The programme will distinguish direct observation from modelling and will not manufacture false comparability.

### Complementarity

RareBurden Commons will work with existing ontologies, registries, genomic programmes, burden studies and public agencies. It will fill cross-system gaps rather than duplicate their mandates.

### Reproducibility and accountability

Methods, transformations, decisions, conflicts and revisions will be versioned. Material changes will be auditable through Git and formal decision records.
