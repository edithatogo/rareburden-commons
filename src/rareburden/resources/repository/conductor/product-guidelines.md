# Product and communication guidelines

## Voice

- Ambitious but not grandiose.
- Constructive toward existing institutions; describe an evidence gap rather than institutional failure.
- Precise about what is observed, inferred, modelled or proposed.
- Accessible to patient leaders and policymakers without diluting technical accuracy.

## Terminology

- Use **people living with a rare disease**, not labels that reduce people to diagnoses.
- Use **rare within common** for rare aetiologies embedded in broad diagnostic categories.
- Use **federated analysis** only when code or approved queries run where data remain.
- Use **public**, **registered**, **controlled**, **partner-generated** and **new collection** as distinct access classes.
- Use **estimate** rather than **count** unless the numerator was directly observed.

## Evidence statements

Every quantitative claim must identify:

- population, geography and period;
- disease/case definition and ontology version;
- source and access class;
- whether the value is observed or modelled;
- uncertainty and material limitations;
- risk of overlap or double counting.

## Visual design

- Prefer simple, high-information graphics.
- Include accessible text summaries with every Mermaid diagram.
- Do not use colour as the sole carrier of meaning.
- Expose uncertainty and missingness rather than visually hiding them.

## External positioning

RareBurden Commons is a proposed public-good measurement layer. It must not imply endorsement, partnership or data access from IHME, WHO, Orphanet, Genomics England, All of Us or any other organisation until formally agreed.
