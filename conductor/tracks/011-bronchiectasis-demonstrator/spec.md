# Track 011 specification — Bronchiectasis rare-aetiology demonstrator

## Objective

Demonstrate rare-within-common estimation when the common phenotype contains multiple rare aetiologies, overlapping diagnoses, age-dependent recognition and heterogeneous outcomes.

## Required outputs

- demonstrator protocol RBC-P003;
- versioned bronchiectasis denominator and rare-aetiology definitions;
- inclusion of relevant categories such as cystic fibrosis, primary ciliary dyskinesia, immunodeficiency and other justified causes;
- overlap and mutually exclusive reporting strategy;
- evidence ledger for aetiologic fractions, diagnosis delay, outcomes and service use;
- public/synthetic reference analysis with uncertainty and sensitivity;
- comparison of alternative case definitions and diagnostic capacity;
- reproducible report and external respiratory/rare-disease review.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Multi-aetiology overlap is represented explicitly and tested.
2. Age, geography and ascertainment differences are not collapsed without justification.
3. The common-disease envelope and rare-aetiology parameters are compatible or appropriately calibrated.
4. Results distinguish observed diagnosis from estimated underlying aetiology.
5. Structural uncertainty from unclassified and multiple causes is visible.
6. The analysis reproduces from release artefacts.
7. Scientific and patient/community review is complete.

## Non-goals

- a clinical diagnostic algorithm;
- assuming all bronchiectasis cohorts share the same aetiologic composition;
- forcing people with multiple contributing conditions into a misleading single cause;
- extrapolating referral-centre estimates without transportability analysis.

## v1 contribution

This track proves that the semantic and burden framework works beyond a relatively discrete monogenic example.

## Non-binding protocol draft — RBC-P003 v0.1.0 (2026-07-27)

Preparatory only; this does not activate the track or freeze bronchiectasis contracts. Define a geography-, period-, age- and setting-specific denominator and estimate rare-aetiology fractions/cases with outcome and service-use scenarios. Represent mutually exclusive, multi-aetiology, unclassified and unknown categories, with overlap and ascertainment uncertainty. Use versioned mappings and ledger parameters, and test referral, diagnostic-capacity and setting transportability scenarios. Respiratory clinical, epidemiology, patient/community and engineering review are required.
