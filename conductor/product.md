# Product definition

## Product

**RareBurden Commons** — the open, reproducible evidence infrastructure of the proposed Global Rare Disease Burden Initiative.

## Problem

Rare diseases are individually uncommon but collectively consequential. Their burden is fragmented across disease registries, genomic cohorts, administrative datasets, surveys, published epidemiology and broad common-disease categories. Existing global health systems generally do not expose a coherent, auditable way to estimate their collective burden or the contribution of rare aetiologies within common syndromes.

## Product promise

Make the collective health, social and economic burden of rare diseases **measurable, comparable and actionable** while preserving local control of sensitive data.

## Primary users

- patient and family organisations;
- ministries of health and public-health agencies;
- clinical, genomic, epidemiological and health-economics researchers;
- rare-disease registries and data custodians;
- global health organisations and burden-of-disease teams;
- funders, payers, HTA bodies and policy analysts;
- country-node analysts and secure-environment operators.

## Stable v1 supported capabilities

1. Versioned rare-disease semantic backbone and burden-purpose hierarchy.
2. Public source, release, access and evidence-gap catalogue.
3. Reproducible public-source acquisition and provenance manifests.
4. Evidence and parameter ledger with quality and transportability.
5. Public burden engine with explicit uncertainty, overlap and non-estimability.
6. Monogenic-diabetes, bronchiectasis and collective paediatric-burden demonstrators.
7. Federated node package for approved local analysis and disclosure-safe outputs.
8. Economic and social burden module.
9. Accessible static atlas, aggregate data package and versioned API contract.
10. Stable schemas, CLI, release evidence, support, correction and migration processes.

## Stability promise

Stable v1 means the supported methods, contracts, release process and bounded outputs are independently reviewable, reproducible, secure and supportable. It does **not** mean complete estimates for every disease or country.

## Non-goals through v1

- building a new international patient registry or central participant-level data lake;
- transferring identifiable records between jurisdictions by default;
- replacing Orphanet, GBD, WHO, national registries or genomic programmes;
- treating allele frequency as disease prevalence without penetrance and ascertainment modelling;
- treating case composition as a DALY, mortality or cost fraction without an explicit outcome model;
- real-time clinical surveillance or individual clinical decision support;
- publishing country rankings that ignore uncertainty, ascertainment and data quality;
- claiming partnership, endorsement or global representativeness beyond documented evidence.

## Guiding principles

Open by default; federated where necessary; patient-governed; locally legitimate; globally oriented but evidence-bounded; methodologically transparent; uncertainty-forward; reproducible; secure; accessible; complementary to existing institutions; and explicit about conflicts and limitations.
