# Product definition

## Product

**RareBurden Commons** — the open, reproducible evidence infrastructure of the proposed Global Rare Disease Burden Initiative.

## Problem

Rare diseases are individually uncommon but collectively consequential. Their burden is fragmented across disease registries, genomic cohorts, administrative datasets, surveys, published epidemiology and broad “common disease” categories. Existing global health systems generally do not expose a coherent, auditable way to estimate their collective burden or the contribution of rare aetiologies within common syndromes.

## Product promise

Make the collective health, social and economic burden of rare diseases **measurable, comparable and actionable** while preserving local control of sensitive data.

## Primary users

- patient and family organisations;
- ministries of health and public-health agencies;
- clinical, genomic and health-economics researchers;
- rare-disease registries and data custodians;
- global health organisations and burden-of-disease teams;
- funders, payers, HTA bodies and policy analysts.

## High-level capabilities

1. A versioned rare-disease semantic backbone and coding crosswalk.
2. A public catalogue of data sources, access routes and evidence gaps.
3. Reproducible public-data burden models with explicit uncertainty.
4. A “rare within common” attribution framework.
5. Federated analytic packages for controlled country and cohort nodes.
6. Policy-facing atlases, country profiles and economic cases.

## Scope for the first programme increment

The first increment establishes the project, public-source catalogue, methodological protocol and one reproducible demonstrator specification. It does not claim to deliver definitive global burden estimates.

## Non-goals

- building a new international patient registry;
- transferring identifiable records into a central data lake;
- replacing Orphanet, GBD, WHO, national registries or genomic programmes;
- treating allele frequency as disease prevalence without penetrance and ascertainment modelling;
- publishing false precision where source data are weak.

## Guiding principles

Open by default; federated where necessary; patient-governed; globally representative; methodologically transparent; uncertainty-forward; reproducible; complementary to existing institutions; and explicit about conflicts of interest.
